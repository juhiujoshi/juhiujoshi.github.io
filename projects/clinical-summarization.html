Multi-agent clinical summarization.

Making longitudinal records easier to review without hiding uncertainty.

A persona-aware summarization workflow that extracts information from heterogeneous reports, generates an audience-specific draft, and uses a separate review agent to identify gaps.

01 / CONTEXT

Start with the problem.

A patient’s history may be spread across pathology, radiology, genomics, biopsy, discharge and consultation documents. The objective was to synthesize that evidence into useful summaries for different clinical readers while preserving a meaningful review step.

My contribution

Worked on agent orchestration, persona-aware generation, feedback/revision logic, evaluation and communication of the workflow.

03 / IMPLEMENTATION

How the approach works.

Concrete technical responsibilities, without private code or configuration details.

01

Source-specific normalization

Different clinical document types were handled as distinct inputs before being consolidated into structured information. This reduced the need for a single prompt to interpret all report formats at once.

02

Reader-aware generation

A context-identification step and reader-specific instructions informed the generated summary. A medical specialist and a patient may need different emphasis from the same underlying evidence.

03

Independent feedback loop

A separate agent reviewed whether expected topics were addressed and provided revision instructions to the summarizer. The review step was intentionally distinct from initial generation.

04 / ENGINEERING REASONING

Decisions & trade-offs.

Why a multi-agent design?

Splitting extraction, summarization and review made responsibilities clearer and exposed intermediate outputs for inspection.

Why persona-specific summaries?

Different readers need different levels of context and terminology; personalization was treated as a presentation decision, not a change to underlying facts.

What makes evaluation difficult?

Coverage alone does not establish clinical correctness. Factual consistency, omissions, hallucinations and specialist review matter together.
